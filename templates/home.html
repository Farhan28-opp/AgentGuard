How it works
  1. 01
    You set a policy — overall authority, a per-purchase limit, allowed merchants.
  2. 02
    Your Main Agent delegates only the authority each task needs.
  3. 03
    AgentGuard checks every signed request: identity, policy and behavioural risk.
  4. 04
    Drunix enforces the reserve and the commit on its ledger when enforcement is on.

    You authorize, or revoke, at any time. The agent never holds your money.

One control layer. Two independent checks.

AgentGuard × Drunix
AgentGuard decides

Is this request acceptable?

  • The agent's Ed25519 signature, nonce, timestamp and payload hash
  • Your policy: limits, allowed merchants and categories, approval mode
  • Behaviour, scored by an IsolationForest: allow, review or contain
Drunix enforces

Does the authority actually exist?

  • The agentauth chaincode re-checks limits, hierarchy, expiry and revocation
  • Reserve, then commit — idempotent, never committed twice
  • With enforcement on, a payment is recorded only after a VALID commit
Drunix is NPCI's private blockchain framework. AgentGuard reaches it through a Fabric Gateway bridge. Open the Drunix Ledger {{ icon("arrow", "icon icon-sm arrow") }}

Four controls stand between an agent and your money.

01 — Identity

Every agent signs what it asks for.

Each agent has its own Ed25519 key. Requests are verified for signature, timestamp, one-time nonce and payload integrity before any authority moves.

02 — Authority

Budgets are delegated, never minted.

Authority moves down the agent hierarchy and is reserved, committed, released or revoked. The ledger always adds up, and no model can override it.

03 — Behaviour

Unusual behaviour is contained.

An IsolationForest scores requests that are already within limits. High risk revokes the agent's authority and releases its holds.

04 — Ledger

Authority is enforced on Drunix.

With enforcement on, every reserve and commit must be committed VALID by the agentauth chaincode, which refuses invalid operations on its own.
